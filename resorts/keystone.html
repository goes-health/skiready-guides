Altitude first

  1. The base here is 9,280 ft. If you can, spend a night around Denver (about 5,000 ft) on the way up.
  2. Make day one a short lesson or half day, with an early bedtime.
  3. Watch for headache, nausea, poor appetite, fussiness or poor sleep in the first 24 hours. Rest and go no higher; get care if it's getting worse.

Make a no-signal plan

  • Pick one easy meeting spot (a named lift base or lodge) and show it to your kids.
  • Put your phone number on their lift pass or a wristband, and take a photo of their outfit in the morning.
  • Teach them to stop at the side of the run and ask a lift operator, instructor or ski patroller for help.

The 3-check ski day at Keystone

  1. Before the first lift: face covered, goggles meet the helmet, one dry sock, mittens on.
  2. At every warm-up break: look at cheeks and noses, feel fingers and toes, swap anything wet.
  3. After lunch: quiet, clumsy or cranky? Make it a shorter afternoon. Hypothermia signs
