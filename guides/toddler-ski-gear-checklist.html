Toddler Ski Gear Checklist for Cold Days (Ages 2–5)

Dressing a toddler for the snow comes down to three things: thin layers you can add or take off, nothing wet against the skin, and every bit of skin covered when the wind picks up. Here's the whole list, from car seat to chairlift.

Updated Nov 26, 2026 · Sources: AAP, CPS, , SickKids

Quick answer

  1. Several thin layers beat one bulky one. Start with a wicking base layer, never cotton.
  2. Mittens, a hat that covers the ears, a neck warmer, and waterproof boots roomy enough to wiggle toes.
  3. Take the puffy coat off before buckling the car seat, and pack a dry spare of everything.

AAPCPS

Why toddlers need a different checklist

Kids lose heat faster than adults because their bodies are smaller, and when they're having fun they don't want to come in. AAP Babies and toddlers are at greater risk of cold injuries because they can't tell you they're cold. SickKids So the gear does the work: dress them so they stay dry and covered, and check on them instead of asking.

The drive up: car seats and coats

Do

  • Dress your toddler in thin layers for the car: leggings or long underwear, pants, a warm top and a thin fleece. aap-carseat
  • Take off the puffy coat or snowsuit before buckling, then lay the coat or a blanket over the harness. aap-carseat
  • Do the pinch test: if you can pinch the harness strap at the chest, tighten it. aap-carseat
  • Put hats, mittens and socks on in the car. They keep kids warm without getting in the way of the straps. aap-carseat

Don't

  • Don't buckle a child in over a winter coat or snowsuit. In a crash the padding flattens and leaves the harness too loose. aap-carseat
  • Don't leave wet mittens on for the ride home. Wet mittens make kids colder, not warmer. aap-carseat

Your pocket kit for warm-up breaks

  • A dry pair of mittens and socks for each child. Change out of anything wet right away. AAP
  • A spare neck warmer or balaclava. They get damp from breath fast.
  • Snacks and a drink. AAP suggests something warm to drink when warming a cold child back up. AAP
  • Tabs on zippers so small hands can work them with mittens on. sickkids-dressing
  • A plan for indoor breaks on a schedule, not just when someone complains. AAP

Getting the fit right on the hill

Take a layer off just before a burst of activity and put it back on when you stop, so kids don't sweat through their base layer. sickkids-dressing If you're standing in a lift line or waiting at the bottom of the magic carpet, get them off the snow and out of the wind when you can. sickkids-dressing At night, hang up jackets and snow pants and dry hats, mittens and boot liners somewhere warm, because damp gear won't keep anyone warm tomorrow. sickkids-dressing And check the wind chill before you go: AAP advises avoiding outdoor play below -15°F, when exposed skin begins to freeze within minutes. Parent FAQ

How many layers should a toddler wear skiing?

Usually three: a wicking base layer, an insulating fleece or wool middle layer, and a waterproof, windproof outer layer. The middle can be two thin pieces so you can adjust (SickKids AboutKidsHealth).

Mittens or gloves for a 3-year-old skier?

Mittens. Kids can bunch their fingers together inside mittens, which keeps them warmer in real cold (Canadian Paediatric Society). Pack a dry spare pair.

Can my toddler wear a snowsuit in the car seat?

No. Bulky coats and snowsuits shouldn't go under a car seat harness. Dress them in thin layers, buckle them in snugly, then put the coat or a blanket over the straps (AAP).

Is cotton OK under a ski suit?

Skip it. Cotton holds moisture, and damp skin loses heat quickly. Choose polyester, silk or similar materials for the base layer (SickKids AboutKidsHealth).
