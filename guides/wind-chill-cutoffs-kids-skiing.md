# How Cold Is Too Cold to Ski with Kids? Wind Chill Cutoffs, Chairlift Breaks & Layering

> Wind chill numbers pediatric groups use (AAP: avoid below -15°F), what the NWS frostbite chart means on a chairlift, how ski schools handle cold days, and how to layer kids. With a quick wind-chill calculator.

Source page: https://skiready.goes.health/guides/wind-chill-cutoffs-kids-skiing
Updated: 2026-11-26

There's no single number every resort uses, but pediatric guidance gives you a clear line. Check the wind chill, not just the temperature, and plan the day around it.

## Quick answer
1. Check the wind chill, not the air temperature. Wind is what freezes cheeks on the lift.
2. Below 5°F (temperature or wind chill), shorten time outside and take more warm-up breaks.
3. Below -15°F wind chill, pediatric groups advise against outdoor play for kids.

Sources: [AAP HealthyChildren.org: Winter safety tips](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx), [Canadian Paediatric Society: Frostbite](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/frostbite), [National Weather Service: Wind chill chart](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart)

## The numbers pediatric groups use

| Temperature or wind chill | What guidance says | Ski-day plan for young kids |
|---|---|---|
| Above 5°F | Normal winter play; dress in layers ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx)) | Normal day with regular indoor warm-up breaks |
| 5°F to -15°F | Cut back time outside ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/frostbite)) | Shorter day, face fully covered, frequent lodge breaks |
| Below -15°F | Avoid outdoor play: exposed skin begins to freeze within minutes ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx)) | Indoor day, or skip the lifts for young kids |
| -16°F (-27°C) or below | Consider keeping kids indoors ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/frostbite)) | Indoor day |

## What the NWS chart means on a chairlift

The National Weather Service wind chill chart shades three frostbite zones for exposed skin: 30 minutes, 10 minutes, and 5 minutes. ([NWS](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart)) For example, 0°F air with a 15 mph wind feels like -19°F, and frostbite can happen in about 30 minutes. ([NWS](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart)) A slow chairlift ride, plus the wind you create skiing down, adds up quickly. That's why covered faces and short laps matter more than how cold the temperature looks. The summit is usually colder and windier than the base, where most forecasts are made.

## Will ski school cancel?

Policies vary by resort and program, and many schools keep teaching on cold days as long as lifts run, with more indoor breaks. Some programs publish a cutoff (one Minnesota kids' program, for example, may cancel at -10°F temperature or wind chill). Call your ski school the day before, ask what they do on cold days, and tell the instructor how your child acts when they're cold. You can always choose a half day.

## Layering that works for kids

- Base: thin wool or synthetic, never cotton (it holds sweat). ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx))
- Middle: fleece or light puffy. Add or remove it to match the day.
- Shell: waterproof jacket and pants; snow in a cuff means wet skin later.
- Face: balaclava or gaiter plus goggles that meet the helmet with no gap. ([SickKids](https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/cold-weather-injuries))
- Hands: mittens, long cuffs, a spare dry pair in your pocket. ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/frostbite))
- Feet: one thin ski sock and boots that let toes wiggle. ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/frostbite))

## Parent FAQ

**How cold is too cold for kids to ski?**
The American Academy of Pediatrics advises avoiding outdoor play when the temperature or wind chill is below -15°F, because exposed skin begins to freeze within minutes. The Canadian Paediatric Society suggests cutting time outside at 5°F (-15°C) or colder.

**How do I calculate wind chill?**
The NWS formula is 35.74 + 0.6215T - 35.75(V^0.16) + 0.4275T(V^0.16), where T is air temperature in °F and V is wind speed in mph. It applies when T is 50°F or less and wind is above 3 mph. Use the calculator on this page.

**Is it colder on the chairlift?**
Usually. The upper mountain tends to be colder and windier than the base, and the lift is exposed. Cover faces and plan shorter laps on cold days.

**Does ski school cancel for cold?**
It depends on the resort and program. Many keep running while lifts run, with extra indoor breaks. Ask your ski school directly and choose a half day if you're unsure.
