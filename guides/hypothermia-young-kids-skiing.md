# Hypothermia in Young Children on the Mountain: Early Signs, Safe Rewarming & When to Call 911

> The early signs of hypothermia in toddlers and young kids on a ski day (shivering, clumsiness, going quiet), how to rewarm safely, and when to call 911. Based on CDC, NIH MedlinePlus and AAP guidance.

Source page: https://skiready.goes.health/guides/hypothermia-young-kids-skiing
Updated: 2026-11-28

Hypothermia on a ski day usually builds slowly: wet layers, a long lesson, a windy lift. The early signs are easy to see once you know them, and dry clothes plus a warm lodge fix most of it.

## Quick answer
1. Shivering is the body's early warning. Head inside, get dry, add layers, and offer a warm drink.
2. Clumsy hands, stumbling, slurred words, unusual quiet or sleepiness: act now.
3. Shivering that stops while they're still cold, confusion, or very low energy: call 911 or ski patrol.

Sources: [CDC: Preventing hypothermia](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html), [MedlinePlus (NIH): Hypothermia](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000038.htm)

## Why young kids get cold faster

Smaller kids lose heat faster and often don't notice they're getting cold while they're having fun. ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx)) Little kids often can't tell you they're cold. Look, don't ask. Babies with hypothermia can have bright red, cold skin and very low energy. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html)) Hypothermia can happen even above 40°F if a child is wet from snow, sweat or rain. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html))

## Symptom check: what to watch for

| Sign | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Shivering, cold hands, saying they're cold or wanting to stop | The body's early warning | Go inside, get dry, add layers, give them a snack and a warm drink ([SickKids](https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/cold-weather-injuries)) |
| Fumbling hands, clumsy skiing, stumbling, slurred or mumbled words | Hypothermia is setting in | Get them warm now: out of wet clothes, into blankets and dry layers, in a warm room ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html)) |
| Unusually quiet, confused, drowsy, very low energy | A serious sign | Get medical help now: ski patrol or 911 ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html)) |
| Shivering stops while they're still cold, slowed breathing | Getting more severe | Call 911 or ski patrol now ([NIH](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000038.htm)) |
| Baby or toddler with bright red, cold skin and very low energy | Hypothermia in infants | Get medical help right away ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html)) |

## Safe rewarming: do and don't

**Do**
- Get into a warm room or shelter; at a resort, that means the lodge or the ski patrol hut. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html))
- Take off wet clothes, including socks and base layers. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html))
- Warm the center of the body first (chest, neck, head and groin) under loose, dry blankets and layers. Skin-to-skin contact helps. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html))
- If they're alert, a warm drink helps. Never give drinks to a child who isn't fully alert. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html))
- Take their temperature if you can. Below 95°F is an emergency: get medical help. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html))

**Don't**
- Don't wait it out on the lift line; go in at the first steady shivering. ([SickKids](https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/cold-weather-injuries))
- Don't put heating pads, heat lamps or a fireplace directly on numb fingers or toes; numb skin burns easily. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html))

## When to call 911

Call 911 (or flag ski patrol, who can reach you faster on the hill) if your child is confused, unusually drowsy or hard to wake, has slurred speech, stops shivering while still cold, has slowed breathing, or has a temperature below 95°F. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html)) ([NIH](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000038.htm)) Hypothermia and frostbite often happen together, so check fingers, toes and cheeks too. ([NIH](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000038.htm))

## Prevention that works for little skiers

- Dry beats warm: swap wet mittens and neck gaiters at lunch, and pack spares. ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx))
- Thin layers (wool or synthetic base, fleece, waterproof shell), no cotton. ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx))
- Indoor warm-up breaks on cold days, before kids ask. ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx))
- Snacks and warm drinks at breaks help kids keep making heat.
- Tell the ski school instructor how your child acts when they're cold.

## Parent FAQ

**What are the first signs of hypothermia in a child?**
Shivering, being very tired, and fumbling hands are early signs. Confusion, memory loss, slurred speech and drowsiness are more serious. Babies can have bright red, cold skin and very low energy (CDC).

**Is it a good sign if my child stops shivering?**
Not if they're still cold. At very low body temperatures shivering can stop (NIH MedlinePlus). If a cold child stops shivering, gets confused or very sleepy, call 911 or ski patrol.

**What temperature is hypothermia in a child?**
CDC advises that a body temperature below 95°F is an emergency that needs immediate medical attention.

**Can kids get hypothermia when it isn't very cold?**
Yes. Hypothermia can happen at cool temperatures above 40°F if someone is wet from rain, sweat or snow and gets chilled (CDC). Wet spring and storm-day skiing count.
