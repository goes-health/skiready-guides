# High-Altitude & Cold Prep for Family Ski Trips (8,000–12,000 ft)

> How altitude affects kids on Colorado and Utah ski trips (Breckenridge 9,600 ft base, Keystone 9,280 ft): signs in toddlers, a night in Denver, hydration, and when to get care. Based on CDC Yellow Book and Children's Hospital Colorado.

Source page: https://skiready.goes.health/guides/altitude-cold-family-ski-trip
Updated: 2026-11-26

Most kids do great at altitude. A slower first day, a night lower down if you can, and knowing the few signs to watch for are all it takes.

## Quick answer
1. Kids get altitude sickness about as often as adults, possibly a little less. Most do fine.
2. If you can, sleep a night around Denver (about 5,000 ft) before a 9,000+ ft resort.
3. Watch for headache, nausea, poor appetite, fussiness or poor sleep in the first 24 hours. Rest, don't go higher, and get care if it isn't improving.

Sources: [Children's Hospital Colorado: Altitude sickness in kids](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/), [CDC Yellow Book: High-altitude travel](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html)

## What to expect at ski-resort altitude

Symptoms usually start 2 to 12 hours after arriving, often during or after the first night. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html)) Children's Hospital Colorado notes that kids are at about the same risk as adults, possibly a little less, and calls it "something to be aware of, but not something to have a paralyzing amount of fear over". ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/)) Bases in this directory range from 1,559 ft (Stowe) to 9,600 ft (Breckenridge), and summits reach 12,998 ft.

## Symptom check for kids who can't say what's wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Headache, nausea, not hungry, trouble sleeping (verbal kids) | Rest, keep them fed, no higher today. Mild symptoms usually ease within 1 to 3 days without going higher ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html)) |
| Fussy, less playful, pale, eating less, sleeping more or less than usual (toddlers) | Treat it like altitude sickness if several happen together ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/)) |
| Symptoms getting worse, or not improving with rest | Get medical help and be ready to go lower ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/)) |
| Trouble breathing at rest, confusion, very hard to wake, clumsy walking | Emergency: call 911 ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html)) |

## A calm plan for the first 48 hours

- Day 0: a night around Denver if you can. ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))
- Above 8,000 ft, avoid climbing more than about 1,600 ft a day where you can (sleeping altitude matters most). ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))
- Day 1: short lesson or half day, easy terrain, early bedtime. ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))
- Drink water regularly, but not more than normal. Encourage eating even if they're not very hungry. ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))
- Kids with a cold or ear infection may be more likely to have symptoms. ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))
- Know where the nearest clinic or hospital is before you need it. ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))

## Cold and altitude together

Higher altitude usually means colder, windier air, and dry mountain air dries kids out over a long day. Plan the same warm-up breaks you would for cold (see the wind chill guide), and keep water and snacks in your pocket. Doctors usually don't recommend taking a newborn under 6 weeks old much higher than where you live. ([Children's Colorado](https://www.childrenscolorado.org/just-ask-childrens/articles/altitude-sickness/))

## Parent FAQ

**Can kids get altitude sickness skiing in Colorado?**
Yes. Children are at about the same risk as adults, possibly a little less (Children's Hospital Colorado). Symptoms usually start 2 to 12 hours after arrival, often after the first night (CDC Yellow Book).

**What are signs of altitude sickness in a toddler?**
Extra fussiness or irritability, less play, pale skin, eating less, and sleeping more or less than usual, especially several at once (Children's Hospital Colorado).

**Should we stay in Denver before Breckenridge?**
If you can, yes. Spending a night around Denver (about 5,000 ft) before going to resorts above 9,000 ft helps the body adjust (Children's Hospital Colorado, Breckenridge Resort).

**When is altitude sickness an emergency?**
Call 911 for trouble breathing at rest, confusion, extreme sleepiness, or clumsy walking. Get care if symptoms worsen or don't improve with rest, and be ready to go lower.
