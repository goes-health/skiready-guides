# Hand & Foot Warmers for Kids: Where They Help and How to Use Them Safely

> How to use disposable hand and toe warmers safely with kids on a ski day: where to put them, why they never go against bare skin or into a tight boot, why they're not for numb or frostbitten skin, and what to do if a child bites one open. Based on Wilderness Medical Society, CDC and poison center guidance.

Source page: https://skiready.goes.health/guides/hand-foot-warmers-kids-safety
Updated: 2026-11-28

Hand and toe warmers are a great extra on a cold ski day, and the Wilderness Medical Society lists them as one way to keep fingers and toes warm. They just need a few rules with kids: always a layer of fabric between warmer and skin, never in a boot that's already snug, and never as a fix for skin that's already numb.

## Quick answer
1. Keep a layer between warmer and skin: in the mitten's outer pocket or on top of the sock, never against bare skin.
2. Don't squeeze a warmer into a tight boot. If it makes the fit snug, it can cut off blood flow and make toes colder.
3. Numb, white or waxy skin needs gentle body warmth and a check, not a warmer. Frostbitten skin can burn without feeling it.

Sources: [Wilderness Medical Society: Frostbite guidelines, 2024 update](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359), [CDC: Preventing frostbite](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html)

## Why warmers help, and why kids need extra care

The Wilderness Medical Society's frostbite guidelines list chemical hand and foot warmers as one way to keep fingers and toes warm in the cold, with two conditions: the warmer must not sit directly against the skin, and it must not make a boot so tight that it cuts off blood flow. ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359)) Kids add a third: they can't always tell you something feels too hot, especially once their hands and feet are cold. ([SickKids](https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/cold-weather-injuries)) So a grown-up places the warmer, checks it at breaks, and takes it out if anything looks red or sore.

## Where warmers go (and where they don't)

| Spot | OK? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Mittens | Yes, with a layer | In a mitten's outer pocket or between a liner and the shell, not loose against the palm. Mittens already keep fingers warmer than gloves ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/frostbite)) |
| Boots | Only if the fit stays roomy | On top of the sock, never inside it. If toes can't wiggle, take it out: a tight boot squeezes blood flow ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359)) ([SickKids](https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/dressing-for-the-cold)) |
| Jacket pockets | Yes | A warm pocket gives cold hands somewhere to go on the chairlift without touching the warmer directly ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359)) |
| Bare skin | No | Warmers must not be placed directly against skin ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359)) |
| Numb or frostbitten skin | No | Frostbitten areas are numb and can easily burn ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html)) |

## A safe routine for ski day

**Do**
- Open and activate warmers indoors or in the car, before they go into cold gear. WMS advises warmers be close to body temperature before they're activated. ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359))
- Put them in yourself, with fabric between the warmer and your child's skin. ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359))
- At every warm-up break, check fingers and toes and look at the skin under where the warmer sat. ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359))
- Keep socks and mittens dry. A warmer can't make up for wet gear, so swap anything damp right away. ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/safety-prevention/at-play/pages/winter-safety.aspx))

**Don't**
- Don't tuck a warmer inside a sock or under a snug boot liner. ([WMS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10806032231222359))
- Don't use a warmer to rewarm skin that is already white, gray or waxy. That's possible frostbite. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html))
- Don't hand a warmer to a toddler who mouths things. The packet holds iron powder. ([Poison Center](https://blog.mdpoison.com/2025/02/28/will-kids-really-eat-that-part-three/))

## If skin is already numb or looks frostbitten

Signs of frostbite include white or grayish-yellow skin, skin that feels unusually firm or waxy, and numbness. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html)) Head inside, take off anything wet, and warm the area with body heat, such as cold fingers in an armpit, or warm (not hot) water that feels comfortable to an unaffected hand. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html)) Don't rub it, and don't use a heating pad, stove, fireplace or other strong heat, because numb skin can easily burn. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html)) Frostbite should be checked by a health care provider. ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html))

## If your child bites or swallows a warmer

- Hand warmers contain iron powder with ingredients like charcoal, salt or vermiculite. ([Poison Center](https://blog.mdpoison.com/2025/02/28/will-kids-really-eat-that-part-three/))
- The contents of a used warmer are mostly rust and aren't expected to cause severe effects; a large amount of unused powder can cause iron toxicity. ([Poison Center](https://blog.mdpoison.com/2025/02/28/will-kids-really-eat-that-part-three/))
- If your child eats any of the contents, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222. ([Poison Center](https://blog.mdpoison.com/2025/02/28/will-kids-really-eat-that-part-three/))
- If your child has trouble breathing, can't be woken, or is turning blue, call 911. ([Poison Center](https://blog.mdpoison.com/2025/02/28/will-kids-really-eat-that-part-three/))

## Parent FAQ

**Are hand warmers safe for toddlers?**
They can be, with an adult in charge: put the warmer where it can't touch bare skin, check under it at breaks, and keep packets away from little ones who put things in their mouths (Wilderness Medical Society; Maryland Poison Center).

**Can I put a toe warmer inside my child's sock?**
No. Warmers shouldn't sit directly against skin, and they shouldn't make a boot tight enough to restrict blood flow. Put it on top of the sock only if toes can still wiggle (Wilderness Medical Society).

**Should I use a hand warmer on frostbite?**
No. Frostbitten skin is numb and can easily burn. Warm it with body heat or warm, not hot, water and have it checked by a health care provider (CDC).

**What if my child ate part of a hand warmer?**
Call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222. Call 911 if your child has trouble breathing, can't be woken, or is turning blue (Maryland Poison Center).
